# Sanctoria Social · Week 1 · Image prompts

Companion to `2026-10-08-week-01.md`. Seven images, **4:5 portrait, 1080 x 1350**, the tallest format both Facebook and Instagram show in feed, so one image serves both posts.

## The decision that makes this set safe

**The spine is the rose, not the rosary.** October is the Month of the Holy Rosary and *rosarium* means rose garden, so roses carry the month without rendering a devotional object that could contradict four live SKUs. Posts 1 to 6 contain no jewelry, no beads, no medal, no cross and no crucifix at all. Only post 7 shows product.

Visual language carried from the 2026-10-06 email header prompts so the brand reads as one thing: overhead or table height, cream on cream, washed linen, morning daylight from the upper left, shadows never darker than `#E3DACB`, one restrained bronze note, no generated text.

Every image leaves a **clear composite zone** for type to be laid on afterwards. Generated lettering always comes out wrong.

## 7 · Wed 14 Oct · The Saint Benedict Cord Rosary

**Read this before running anything.**

The creative expert's honest verdict: **text alone cannot render this product faithfully.** The construction is unusual in exactly the way that broke the necklace on 2026-09-09. Knotted braided cord rather than a chain. Silver medals interleaved along the full length rather than at one point. An oval Saint Benedict centerpiece where every model's prior wants a Guadalupe oval or a Miraculous Medal. A wood crucifix with a silver corpus plus INRI plus an inset medal, four separate features on one small part.

From text alone a generator will produce lava stone or glass beads, a metal chain, a Marian centerpiece and an ornate all-metal crucifix. **That is four contradictions on a live SKU, on a page the customer can open in the next click.**

### 7A · Recommended. No generation at all.

Use a real supplier photograph. All three verified present:

| File | Size |
|---|---|
| `site/images/rosary-1.jpg` | 695 x 695 |
| `site/images/rosary-2.jpg` | 695 x 695 |
| `site/images/rosary-3.jpg` | 695 x 695 |

Place the photo on a 1080 x 1350 cream `#F7F3EC` canvas, product mass in the lower 55 percent, slightly left of centre, soft feathered shadow no darker than `#E3DACB`, upper 40 percent clean for one line of type in bronze `#9A7B4F`.

**Note the source is only 695px.** Keep the product at or near native size inside the canvas, occupying about 64 percent of the width, rather than upscaling it to fill. Upscaling 1.5x on a product photo will show.

**Check the crop for supplier branding.** A visible card, sticker or third-party wording is the exact mistake `pair-3` shipped with on the live necklace page.

Twenty minutes, guaranteed accurate, and "quiet product presence" is precisely the brief where a plain real photograph on a cream field beats a styled render.

### 7B · Only if a staged scene is genuinely wanted

Run as a **reference-conditioned generation with rosary-1, rosary-2 and rosary-3 all three attached**. Per the 2026-09-09 learning, the references are what make an unusual construction renderable at all. **Do not run this without them.**


## GENERATOR NOTES

Written for **Higgsfield `nano_banana_pro`**, already proven here. It handles long declarative prompts with percentage placement and hex values better than anything else in the stack, and it accepts attached references, which 7B structurally depends on.

- **Aspect.** Request 4:5 directly. If the build only offers 2:3 or 3:4 portrait, render the nearest taller ratio and crop 1080 x 1350 from the centre. Every percentage is written for the **final** 4:5 frame, so crop first, then verify the composite zone with a picker.
- **It renders text unprompted**, worst on paper, cloth and ceramics. **Post 6 is a notebook made of paper**, the highest text-risk image in the set. Keep the `CRITICAL: no text` block in the positive prompt, not just the negative. If it keeps writing on the pages after about six seeds, close the notebook and shoot it shut with the pencil on top. The invitation weakens but the asset ships.
- **Never use absolute units.** Every size is anchored to an in-frame ratio, per the 2026-09-09 LEARNINGS entry. Millimetres came back at roughly double true size on the necklace and cost eleven regenerations.
- **7B needs all three references.** Without them it invents lava stone and a Guadalupe oval. With them, expect two to four passes on the crucifix, the most feature-dense small part in the catalogue.

Elsewhere: **Midjourney** ignores hex, use named colours, add `--ar 4:5 --style raw --s 50`, move the negative into `--no`, budget more seeds because clear zones are not reliably respected, and for post 7 use `--cref` or `--sref` with a real photo or do not run it. **Flux and Ideogram** both use hex; Ideogram tries hardest to add text, so disable text rendering rather than negative-prompting it. **DALL-E 3** rewrites prompts and adds props: halve each one, lead with the clear zone, and never use it for post 7 since it cannot be reference-conditioned.

## RISKS, NAMED

1. **Post 7 is the real risk, and it is the 2026-09-09 risk repeating.** Run 7A with a real photo, or 7B with all three references. Never 7B bare.
2. **Window muntins form crosses.** One Sanctoria product is a plain Latin cross with no Christ figure, so an accidental background cross made of glazing bars reads as deliberate and shows the wrong cross. Banned by name in prompts 1 and 4 and in the shared negative. Easy to miss at thumbnail, obvious at full size.
3. **Post 4 is hands.** Approve at 100 percent with a finger count, never from the grid. If three or four seeds fail, crop the hands out and keep the cup, the cuff and the light. The post survives it.
4. **Post 6 will try to write on the pages.** Budget seeds.
5. **The Marian blue in post 3 is the only colour outside the palette all week.** If it renders bright, royal, navy or cool it will look like a different brand in the Page grid. Reject and re-render rather than desaturating in post, which goes grey.
6. **Cream on cream can go grey.** A white balance problem, not a composition one. Sample the clear zone with a picker. More than about two percent toward blue reads dirty and the composited bronze type will look pasted on.
7. **Posts 2, 5 and 6 are deliberately near-empty** and will feel underwhelming bare. They are type-led and the image is a ground, not a subject. **Judge them with the words laid in** or you will reject the three best layouts in the set.
8. **Roses drift into wedding, florist or skincare.** Keep them garden-grown, slightly past peak, one or two loosened petals, no crisp commercial perfection.
9. **Posts 1 to 6 show no product, which is a real and deliberate cost.** Six posts of warmth that sell nothing, traded for zero chance of showing a devotion the brand does not sell.
10. **No image makes a claim.** No certificates, no seals, no Rome, no clergy, no abbey, no altar, nothing implying a conferred blessing, nothing asserting anything about the viewer. The only words on any of them are composited afterwards, so **the copy remains the sole compliance surface**.

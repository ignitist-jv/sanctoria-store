# The Covenant Restored Workbook

## Seven Movements to Renew Your Family's Covenant With God

*Sanctoria*

---

# How to Use This Workbook

Dear sister in Christ,

If you are holding this workbook, something in you already knows the truth: a home is not held together by effort alone. It is held together by grace.

You have watched over your family for years. You have prayed at kitchen sinks and in car lines and in the dark hours when everyone else was asleep. You have carried worries you never said out loud: a child who no longer goes to Mass, a marriage that has grown quiet, a grandchild being raised without the faith you hold so dear. And somewhere along the way, you may have started to wonder whether the covenant your family once had with God has quietly loosened.

Here is the good news, and it is the whole reason this workbook exists: a covenant with God is never broken from His side. It only waits to be renewed from ours.

This is not a book to read. It is a book to work. Over seven movements, you and God will do something together. You will remember what a covenant is. You will name honestly where the threads have frayed. You will build a simple, daily rhythm of prayer with your St. Benedict Marian Bracelet. You will intercede, room by room and person by person, for the people you love most. And at the end, you will seal a renewed covenant that you can return to for the rest of your life.

**What you will need:**
- Your St. Benedict Marian Bracelet
- This workbook and a pen
- A quiet place, even ten minutes of it
- An honest and open heart

**How each movement works:**
1. Read the teaching slowly. Let it settle before you move on.
2. Sit with the Scripture. Read it twice.
3. Do the writing. This is where the real work happens. Do not skip it.
4. Pray the prayer aloud. Your voice matters.
5. Practice with your bracelet, touching the beads as you pray.
6. Speak the Covenant Statement out loud to close.

**A gentle word before you begin.** You do not have to do this perfectly. If a movement takes you two days, take two days. If you cry while you write, let yourself cry. If you feel resistance, a quiet urge to close the book and tell yourself it will not matter, that resistance is often the sign that something important is about to move.

You are not starting from nothing. You are returning to a covenant that has been waiting for you all along.

Let us begin.

*Crux sacra sit mihi lux. Non draco sit mihi dux.*

# Movement 1: The Covenant Remembered

### Theme: Remembering Who Holds Your Home

---

### Teaching

Before you can restore a covenant, you have to remember that you have one.

A covenant is not a contract. A contract is an exchange, careful and conditional: I will do this if you do that. A covenant is something deeper and older. It is a binding of persons, a promise that says I am yours and you are mine, and that stays true even when one side forgets.

From the very beginning, God has dealt with families through covenant. He made a covenant with Noah and his household. With Abraham and his descendants. With David and his line. And through Christ, He made a covenant sealed not in the blood of animals but in the blood of the Cross, offered for you and for every soul under your roof. When you were baptized, you were brought inside that covenant. When your children were baptized, so were they. The promise was spoken over your whole household.

That is why a mother's prayers carry such weight. You are not begging a distant God for favors. You are reminding a faithful God of a covenant He Himself made. When you pray for your children, you are standing inside a promise. When you pray for your marriage, you are standing on holy ground that was consecrated long ago.

St. Benedict understood the power of a household bound to God. He gathered scattered, restless men and formed them into communities anchored by prayer, by a rule of life, by a shared devotion that held them steady when the world around them fell apart. Your home is your community. Your bracelet is a thread that ties your daily hours back to that same steadiness. Today you begin by simply remembering: this family belongs to God, and God does not forget His own.

---

### Scripture

**Scripture:** Joshua 24:15

*"But as for me and my household, we will serve the Lord."*

Joshua did not say he hoped his household would serve the Lord. He declared it. There is a difference between hoping and declaring. Hoping waits. Declaring plants a flag. Today you plant the flag again.

---

### Reflect and Write

Open to a blank space in this workbook or a journal, and answer honestly:

1. When has your family felt closest to God? Describe that season. What were your days like? What was different?
2. Who first handed you the faith? A mother, a grandmother, a priest, a friend? Write their name and one thing they taught you.
3. What promise do you most want God to keep over your family right now?

Now write this sentence in your own hand and sign it: *"My family belongs to God. That covenant has never been canceled, and today I remember it."*

---

### Prayer

Heavenly Father,

You have kept covenant with families since the beginning of the world. You kept it with Noah, with Abraham, with David, and through Your Son You made it new for me and for everyone I love.

I come to You today not as a stranger asking a favor, but as a daughter standing inside a promise. I remember that my home belongs to You. I remember that the waters of baptism were poured over this family. I remember that You do not forget Your own.

Where I have grown forgetful, remind me. Where I have grown weary, renew me. Through the intercession of St. Benedict, who built steadfast homes of prayer, help me build one too.

In the name of Jesus Christ. Amen.

---

### Bracelet Practice

Hold the St. Benedict Marian Bracelet, or rest your fingers on the beads at your wrist. Move slowly through the beads and, at each one, speak the name of one person in your household, living or gone. With each name, say quietly: *"Lord, this one is Yours."*

When you reach the medal, hold it and pray: *"St. Benedict, watch over this house. Keep us within the covenant of Christ. Amen."*

---

### Covenant Statement

Speak this aloud:

**I remember the covenant. My family belongs to God, and God has never let go. Today I take my place inside His promise. As for me and my household, we will serve the Lord.**

# Movement 2: Naming What Has Drifted

### Theme: Honest Eyes, Open Heart

---

### Teaching

You cannot restore what you will not name.

Drift is quiet. It almost never announces itself. No family decides one morning to grow distant from God. It happens the way a boat leaves a shore: slowly, gently, one small current at a time, until one day you look up and the shore is far away and you cannot quite remember when you last felt it under your feet.

Maybe it began with a Sunday missed for a good reason, and then another. Maybe grace before meals grew shorter and then disappeared. Maybe a child stopped coming to Mass and you stopped asking, because the asking led to arguments and you were tired. Maybe the marriage that once prayed together slowly stopped, and now the silence around faith is one more silence in a house that has too many.

This is not a movement about blame. God is not standing over you with a list of failures. He is standing beside you, offering to help you see clearly, because clear sight is the beginning of every homecoming. The father of the prodigal son did not pretend his son had never left. He saw the road, and he watched it, and he ran the moment his son appeared. Honesty about the distance is what keeps the porch light on.

Today you take honest inventory. Not to condemn yourself or anyone else, but to see the shore clearly again so you know which way to row.

---

### Scripture

**Scripture:** Luke 15:17

*"When he came to himself, he said, How many hired servants of my father have bread enough and to spare, and I perish with hunger."*

The prodigal's return began the moment he saw his situation honestly. Clear sight was the first step home. It is your first step too.

---

### Reflect and Write

Take your time here. This is tender work.

1. Where has faith grown quiet in your home? List the practices that have faded: Mass, prayer before meals, prayer with the bracelet, feast days, confession, family blessings.
2. Which people in your family feel farthest from God right now? Write each name.
3. When you notice the drift, what do you usually feel? Guilt? Grief? Helplessness? Name the feeling honestly.
4. Is there a moment you can point to when things began to loosen? A move, a loss, a busyness, a wound? Describe it.

Now, beside each thing you named, write one small word: *"reachable."* Because it is. Nothing you have written is beyond the reach of grace.

---

### Prayer

Lord,

Give me honest eyes and a gentle heart. Let me see clearly where my family has drifted, without drowning in guilt or losing hope.

I bring You the practices we let go of. I bring You the names of those who feel far away. I bring You the quiet that has settled over the parts of our life that once belonged so plainly to You.

I am not here to condemn myself or anyone I love. I am here to see the shore again. Show me the distance so I can begin to close it, one prayer, one day, one small return at a time.

Through the intercession of St. Benedict, steady my hands for the work ahead. In the name of Jesus Christ. Amen.

---

### Bracelet Practice

Hold your bracelet. For each practice or person you named as having drifted, move to one bead and pray: *"Lord, begin the return here."* Do not rush. Let each bead hold one honest concern.

Close by holding the medal: *"St. Benedict, you called wanderers home. Intercede for mine."*

---

### Covenant Statement

Speak this aloud:

**I have seen the drift, and I am not afraid of it. Nothing I have named is beyond the reach of God. Today the return begins. The porch light is on, and it will stay on.**

# Movement 3: The Household Altar

### Theme: Building a Daily Rhythm of Prayer

---

### Teaching

Every steadfast home has a center. A place, and a rhythm, where the family turns back to God.

For centuries, Catholic families kept a household altar: a small corner with a crucifix, a candle, an image of Our Lady, perhaps a saint whose protection the family loved. It was not grand. It did not need to be. It was simply a visible reminder that God lived here, that this home had a heart, and that the heart was prayer.

You do not need a special room. You need a spot and a moment. A shelf, a windowsill, the corner of a dresser. A candle, a holy image, and your St. Benedict Marian Bracelet resting where you will see it. And you need a time, ideally the same time each day, when you go there and pray. Morning is best, before the day pulls you in a hundred directions, but any faithful time is holy.

This is the great secret St. Benedict left the Church: steadiness beats intensity. He did not ask his monks for dramatic feats. He asked them for a rule, a rhythm, prayer at appointed hours, day after day, year after year. That quiet consistency built something no storm could topple. Your ten minutes each morning, kept faithfully, will do more for your family than one desperate hour of prayer in a crisis. The rhythm is the strength.

Today you build the center. You choose the place. You choose the time. And you begin.

---

### Scripture

**Scripture:** Psalm 127:1

*"Except the Lord build the house, they labour in vain that build it."*

You have labored for your family for years. Today you invite the Lord back to the center of the building.

---

### Reflect and Write

1. Where in your home could you keep a small place of prayer? Name the spot.
2. What will you place there? A crucifix, a candle, an image of Mary, your bracelet? List what you have.
3. What time of day can you protect for prayer? Be realistic. Ten faithful minutes beats an hour you will never keep.
4. What usually gets in the way of daily prayer for you? Name it, so you can plan around it.

Now write your simple rule of life in one sentence: *"Each day at ______, I will go to ______ and pray with my bracelet for my family."* Sign it.

---

### Prayer

Father,

Unless You build this house, I labor in vain. So today I invite You to the center of my home and the center of my day.

I set aside a place for You. I set aside a time. Not out of duty, but out of love, and out of the wisdom that has finally taught me I cannot hold this family together on my own.

Help me keep this rhythm when I am tired, when I am busy, when the day fights me for the ten minutes I have promised You. Let this small faithfulness become the steadiest thing in my home.

Through the intercession of St. Benedict, master of holy rhythm, teach my hands to keep the hours. In the name of Jesus Christ. Amen.

---

### Bracelet Practice

Go now, if you can, to the place you chose. Set your bracelet there. Light a candle if you have one. Pray one Our Father, resting your fingers on the beads, and then say: *"This is our place. This is our time. Lord, meet us here."*

From today, let your bracelet live in this spot so that seeing it becomes an invitation to pray.

---

### Covenant Statement

Speak this aloud:

**My home has a center again, and the center is prayer. I will keep the rhythm even when it is small, because steadiness is strength. Unless the Lord builds this house, I labor in vain. So I build it with Him.**

# Movement 4: Interceding for Your Children

### Theme: Standing in the Gap for the Ones You Love

---

### Teaching

There is no prayer a mother prays more often, or more helplessly, than the prayer for a child who has wandered.

It does not matter if they are ten or fifty. When a child steps away from the faith, when they stop going to Mass, when they raise your grandchildren without baptism, when they make choices that frighten you, a particular ache settles in that nothing else touches. You cannot argue them back. You have tried. The arguments only build walls. And so you are left with the one thing that was always more powerful than argument anyway: intercession.

To intercede means to stand in the gap. To place yourself, in prayer, between the person you love and the distance that has opened up, and to hold that space with faith until grace does what your words never could. Monica prayed for her son Augustine for seventeen years while he wandered far from God. She did not lecture him home. She wept and prayed and trusted, and Augustine became one of the greatest saints the Church has ever known. Her tears, a bishop told her, could not be lost.

Your prayers cannot be lost either. Every bead you move for a wandering child is a candle set in the window. You may not see the light reach them. You may not be there when they turn back. But the God who keeps covenant with your household is working in their life even now, especially in the places you cannot see. Your job is not to fix them. Your job is to keep the light burning.

Today you stand in the gap.

---

### Scripture

**Scripture:** Luke 15:20

*"But when he was yet a great way off, his father saw him, and had compassion, and ran, and fell on his neck, and kissed him."*

The father was watching the road. He was ready to run. That is the God you are entrusting your children to today.

---

### Reflect and Write

1. Name each child, grandchild, or family member you long to see return to the faith. Write them one by one.
2. Beside each name, write one thing you love about them. Intercession grows from love, not fear.
3. What do you most fear for them? Write it, then draw a line through it, and above it write: *"Given to God."*
4. What is one bridge you could keep open with them? A call, a visit, a note that carries no lecture, only love?

Now write a short blessing for each name, one line each: *"May ______ come home to the faith, in God's time and by God's hand."*

---

### Prayer

Father of the prodigal, You never stopped watching the road. You never closed the door. You never took back the inheritance. You waited, and when Your child appeared, You ran.

I bring You my children today. [Speak their names.] Wherever they are, whatever they are doing, meet them there. Soften what has hardened. Draw them by roads I cannot see. Put people in their path who carry Your light. Protect my grandchildren, and make a way for them to know You.

I release my fear for them into Your hands, because fear was never the same as love, and love is what I want to give them. I will keep the light in the window. You do the calling home.

Through the intercession of St. Benedict, and through the tears of St. Monica who prayed her son all the way to sainthood, hear a mother's prayer today. In the name of Jesus Christ. Amen.

---

### Bracelet Practice

Hold your bracelet and pray one set of beads with the names of your children on your lips, one name per bead if you can, returning to the same names as often as needed. Each bead is a candle in the window.

Close by holding the medal: *"St. Benedict, guard the ones I have named. Bring them home."*

---

### Covenant Statement

Speak this aloud:

**I stand in the gap for my children. I will not lecture them home; I will pray them home. My prayers cannot be lost. The God who watches the road is watching for them now, and the light in my window will not go out.**

# Movement 5: The Marriage and the Bonds of Home

### Theme: Strengthening the Ties That Hold

---

### Teaching

A home is woven from bonds, and the enemy of every home is not conflict. It is distance.

If you are married, you know how quietly a marriage can grow cool. Not through some dramatic rupture, but through years of two people slowly turning their attention elsewhere, until you are living side by side and somehow alone. The prayers you once said together stopped. The conversations grew practical and thin. The tenderness did not die so much as it fell asleep. And underneath it all runs a loneliness that is harder to bear because the person is right there.

Scripture gives us a beautiful image: a cord of three strands. You, your spouse, and God, woven together. When all three strands are present, the cord holds against almost anything. But pull out the strand of God, let prayer and faith drain out of the marriage, and the remaining two strands fray far more easily. The strengthening of a marriage almost always begins by weaving God back into the center of it.

And if you are widowed, or single, or carrying this alone, hear this clearly: the bonds of your home are no less real. Your bond with your children, your siblings, your dearest friends, your parish family, these are threads God cherishes. Loneliness is one of the heaviest weights a faithful woman carries, and the same God who designed us for one another is at work to knit you back into warm company.

Today you tend the bonds. You invite God back into the center of the ties that hold your home together.

---

### Scripture

**Scripture:** Ecclesiastes 4:12

*"And if one prevail against him, two shall withstand him; and a threefold cord is not quickly broken."*

The third strand is God. Today you weave Him back into the center of every bond you hold.

---

### Reflect and Write

1. Name the closest bonds in your life: your spouse, your children, your siblings, your dearest friends.
2. Which of these bonds feels strong right now? Give thanks for it in writing.
3. Which feels distant or cool? Be honest. What do you think pulled the strand loose?
4. What is one small, warm gesture you could offer this week, with no agenda attached? A kind word, a shared meal, a prayer said together, a hand held?

If you are married, write one sentence asking God back into the center of your marriage. If you carry your home alone, write one sentence asking God to knit you into warmer company.

---

### Prayer

Lord,

You wove us for one another. You said it is not good to be alone. And yet distance has crept into the bonds I hold most dear, and some days I have felt lonely even in a full house.

I bring You my marriage, if I am married, and I ask You to be the third strand again, woven back into the center of it. Soften what has hardened. Wake what has fallen asleep. Give us the grace to pray together again.

I bring You every bond of my home: my children, my family, my friends. Where a thread has loosened, draw it tight. Where I am isolated, bring me warm and faithful company. Let no one under Your covenant be left to carry life alone.

Through the intercession of St. Benedict, who bound strangers into a family of faith, restore the ties of my home. In the name of Jesus Christ. Amen.

---

### Bracelet Practice

Hold your bracelet. For each close bond you named, move to a bead and pray: *"Lord, be the strand that holds this one strong."* If you are married, spend an extra bead simply asking God to be present in your marriage today.

Close by holding the medal: *"St. Benedict, weave my home together in Your peace."*

---

### Covenant Statement

Speak this aloud:

**The bonds of my home are woven with a third strand, and that strand is God. Distance will not have the last word. I invite the Lord into the center of every tie I hold, and a threefold cord is not quickly broken.**

# Movement 6: Blessing the Generations

### Theme: A Legacy of Faith

---

### Teaching

The covenant you renew today is not only for you. It reaches backward and forward, generation upon generation.

Look at your family line the way God does, and you will see the faith handed down like a lamp, from one careful pair of hands to the next. Somewhere behind you is a grandmother who knelt, a mother who lit candles, a father who insisted on Mass. The lamp reached you. And now you hold it. What you do with it will touch children and grandchildren you may never watch grow up.

This is a weighty and beautiful thing. Scripture tells us that while the weight of the fathers' faults may touch a few generations, God's mercy runs a thousand generations deep for those who love Him and keep His commandments. Read that again. A few generations of struggle, against a thousand generations of mercy. The math is overwhelmingly in your favor. You are not fighting an unbeatable inheritance. You are carrying an unstoppable one.

Your prayers today plant seeds in soil you will not stand on. The grandchild who is being raised without the faith may one day feel a strange pull toward a God someone prayed for them to know. The great-grandchild not yet born may inherit a covenant you renewed in a quiet room with a bracelet in your hand. St. Benedict built with eternity in mind; his rule still forms souls fifteen centuries later. Build the same way. Bless the generations.

Today you become the one who hands the lamp forward on purpose.

---

### Scripture

**Scripture:** Deuteronomy 7:9

*"Know therefore that the Lord thy God, he is God, the faithful God, which keepeth covenant and mercy with them that love him and keep his commandments to a thousand generations."*

Mercy to a thousand generations. That is the inheritance you are passing down today.

---

### Reflect and Write

1. Who handed the faith down to you? Trace it back as far as you can. Write their names as a small family litany of faith.
2. What blessing do you most want to pass to your children and grandchildren? Not money, not property. What of the soul?
3. Name each grandchild, or each child not yet born that you hope for. Write a one-line blessing over each.
4. What is one way you could actively hand the lamp forward this year? A gift of a bracelet or a holy image, a story of your faith told aloud, a feast day kept together, a prayer taught?

Now write this over your whole line, backward and forward: *"The mercy of God runs a thousand generations deep in this family, and I am handing it on."*

---

### Prayer

Almighty and faithful God,

You keep covenant and mercy to a thousand generations. I stand today in the middle of a long line of souls, some behind me who handed me the faith, and some ahead of me who will receive it from my hands.

I thank You for the ones who prayed me here. [Name them.] I ask You to bless the ones who come after. [Name your children and grandchildren.] Let the lamp of faith pass into their hands. Let my prayers reach children not yet born. Let this family be known in every generation as a people who belong to You.

Where there has been a pattern of hardship or forgetting, let Your mercy be greater. A few generations of struggle are no match for a thousand generations of grace.

Through the intercession of St. Benedict, who built for centuries he would never see, help me build a legacy of faith. In the name of Jesus Christ. Amen.

---

### Bracelet Practice

Hold your bracelet. Move through the beads praying, first backward, one bead for each person you can name who handed you the faith, giving thanks. Then forward, one bead for each child and grandchild, giving blessing.

Close by holding the medal: *"St. Benedict, let the faith of this family run a thousand generations deep."*

---

### Covenant Statement

Speak this aloud:

**I am a keeper of the lamp. The faith that reached me will not stop with me. God keeps mercy to a thousand generations, and I am handing that mercy forward, to my children, my grandchildren, and every generation still to come.**

# Movement 7: Sealing the Covenant

### Theme: A Rule of Life to Keep

---

### Teaching

Six movements ago, you began by remembering that your family belongs to God. Today you seal it.

You have remembered the covenant. You have named the drift honestly. You have built a place and a rhythm of prayer. You have stood in the gap for your children, tended the bonds of your home, and blessed the generations behind you and ahead of you. That is real work, holy work, the kind most people never sit down to do. Today you gather it all into a single, lasting commitment.

Understand what a sealed covenant is and is not. It is not a promise that life will now be easy. It is not a guarantee that every child returns next month or every strained bond mends by spring. It is something steadier than that. It is a settled decision that, whatever comes, this home will keep turning back to God, day after day, with a bracelet in hand and a covenant in the heart.

This is the wisdom St. Benedict gave the Church: not a burst of fervor, but a rule of life. A simple, repeatable rhythm that holds you through every season, the bright ones and the hard ones. A house cleaned once will gather dust again unless someone keeps it. The covenant renewed today stays renewed through a daily practice, ten faithful minutes, kept for the rest of your life.

Today you write your rule, you seal your covenant, and you step into a home that has its center back.

---

### Scripture

**Scripture:** Philippians 4:6-7

*"Be careful for nothing; but in every thing by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known unto God. And the peace of God, which passeth all understanding, shall keep your hearts and minds through Christ Jesus."*

The peace that guards your heart is not the peace of solved problems. It is the peace of a soul that has handed everything to God. That is the peace you seal today.

---

### Reflect and Write

This is your most important writing. Take your time.

Write your Family Covenant. Copy the lines below in your own hand, filling in the blanks, and sign and date it. Keep it with your bracelet.

*"I, ______, renew my family's covenant with God.*

*I will keep a daily rhythm of prayer with my St. Benedict Marian Bracelet, at ______ each day.*

*I will intercede faithfully for my children and grandchildren, and I will keep the light in the window for any who have wandered.*

*I will invite God back into the center of the bonds of my home.*

*I will hand the faith forward to the generations who come after me.*

*Whatever comes, this home will keep turning back to God.*

*As for me and my household, we will serve the Lord."*

Signed: ______  Date: ______

---

### Prayer

Father,

I come to You on this seventh movement with a full heart. You have walked me back through everything I love: my family, my marriage, my children, the generations behind me and ahead. And in each place, You were already there, keeping covenant, waiting to be remembered.

I seal this covenant today. Not because my family is perfect, but because You are faithful. Not because every worry is resolved, but because I have finally handed them all to You.

Give me the grace to keep the daily rhythm I have promised. Let my ten faithful minutes become the steadiest thing in my home. And let the peace that passes understanding guard my heart and the hearts of everyone I have prayed for.

Through the intercession of St. Benedict, seal this covenant and keep it. In the name of Jesus Christ. Amen.

*Crux sacra sit mihi lux. Non draco sit mihi dux.*

---

### Bracelet Practice

Pray your fullest set of beads today, one movement of the workbook held in mind for each portion: the covenant remembered, the drift named, the household altar, your children, the bonds of your home, the generations, and the sealing. Move slowly. This is a homecoming.

Close by holding the medal and speaking the final blessing: *"St. Benedict, faithful servant of God, keep this covenant, guard this home, and pray for my family before the throne of God, all the days of my life. Amen."*

---

### Covenant Statement

Stand, if you are able. Hold your bracelet. Speak this with your whole voice:

**The covenant is sealed. My family belongs to God. I will keep the daily rhythm. I will pray my children home. I will weave God into every bond of my home. I will hand the faith forward. Whatever comes, this home will keep turning back to God. As for me and my household, we will serve the Lord. Now and always. Amen.**

# A Final Word

You did something rare this week. You did not simply worry about your family, the way most of us do. You sat down, opened your heart, and did the quiet work of renewing a covenant.

Here is how to keep it.

**Keep the daily rhythm.** Ten minutes each morning with your bracelet, at your household altar, praying for the people you love. This is the whole secret. Steadiness beats intensity. The rhythm is the strength.

**Return to this workbook.** When a season grows hard, when a child seems farther away than ever, when the bonds of home feel thin, come back and work a movement again. There is no limit on how many times you can renew a covenant. Each time, you will go a little deeper.

**Trust the slow work of grace.** Some of the seeds you planted this week are buried deep. You may not see them break the surface for months or years. Pray anyway. Bless anyway. Keep the light in the window. The God who keeps covenant to a thousand generations is not in a hurry, and He does not forget.

You are the heart of your home. And a heart that keeps turning back to God can hold a whole family steady.

May the Holy Cross be your light. May the peace of God, which surpasses all understanding, guard your heart and the hearts of everyone you love, now and always.

Amen.

Worn, Not Stored

You ask for protection every morning.
Then you leave it on the dresser.

You say the prayer before the house wakes up. Kitchen table. First cup of coffee. The names you always say.

Then the day starts. Keys, purse, the drive, the list, the phone. By ten o'clock the prayer is behind you.

That is not small faith. That is a busy day. A prayer you have to remember is a prayer you will sometimes forget, and by supper you cannot even say which hour it slipped.

The problem was never how much you believe. It is that every holy thing you own has to be picked up, carried and put back, and a day like yours does not leave a free hand.

This is the one you do not have to remember.

Why Three Medals

One for morning, one for noon, one for night

For more than fifteen centuries, families have turned to Saint Benedict when they wanted their people covered. His medal has been set into the foundations of houses, hung over cribs, tucked above car visors and carried in the pockets of soldiers. It is the one the Church has always reached for when a mother says protect them.

This bracelet carries three of them, detailed and gold tone, spaced along a cord you never take off. Not one you might glance at. Three, across the turn of your wrist, so that whichever way your hand falls during the day, one of them is facing you.

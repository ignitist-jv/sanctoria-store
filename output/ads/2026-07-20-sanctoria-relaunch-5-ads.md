## Phase 2 Strategic Diagnosis

- **Awareness level:** Problem Aware to Solution Aware. She feels the heaviness and has prayed for years; she is looking for a way in that finally holds, not for an unknown problem.
- **Sophistication:** 4 to 5. She has seen devotional offers before. Lead with mechanism and story, not a bare promise.
- **Lead type:** mechanism-led story / PAS (Problem, Agitate, Solution). Open on the emotional problem, then name why this bracelet works differently.
- **Problem mechanism:** the same quiet heaviness repeating down the family line. Children drifting from the faith, a peace that keeps slipping. A pattern she never chose but keeps carrying.
- **Solution mechanism:** the blessed St Benedict Marian bracelet worn daily as the visible sign that the line changes with her. St Benedict medal plus Marian intercession plus a Benedictine blessing, worn and inherited. A sacramental sign carried every day, an aid to intercession, not superstition.
- **Dominant emotion:** protective love hardening into resolve. "It stops with me."
- **One prospect (Teresa):** 58, Catholic grandmother. 6am coffee before the house wakes. Her daughter stopped going to Mass; the grandkids are growing up without the faith. "I don't want them carrying what I carried. Let it stop with me." Believes in the protective power of prayer, saints, and sacred objects. Moved by protective love and a desire for peace.
- **Verbatim phrases (use naturally):** "the cycle stops with me", "It stops with me", praying for the same loved one for 10+ years with no answer, what she carried without complaining, weight in your hand, kitchen table, grandmother, daughter, granddaughter, blessed, intercession, peace first.
- **Objections to preempt:**
  1. Distrust of sellers. Answer: blessed by Benedictine monks at the abbey, offering framing (grace is never sold; the offering covers materials and craftsmanship, the blessing is given freely), 30-day full refund including shipping.
  2. "Why would an object change what years of prayer did not?" Answer: it is a sacramental sign carried daily, an aid to intercession that keeps the prayer close and constant. Not superstition.

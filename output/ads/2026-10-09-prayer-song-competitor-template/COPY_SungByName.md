# Prayer Song, batch 2: the competitor template, rebuilt honestly
Date: 2026-10-09. Angle: SungByName (six-pack) + PrayedForYears (long UGC).
Destination: https://sanctoriastore.com/prayer-song/

## Why batch 1 was weak

Batch 1 was a product description. The competitor's winners are a GIFT ad with a
recipient in the first five words. Three differences, in order of how much they cost us:

1. No recipient. Theirs names the person in word three or four ("his wife", "my daughter").
   Ours named nobody, so nobody self-selected.
2. No gift frame. Theirs ends on "gift 🎁". Ours ended on a mechanism.
3. No emotional register. Theirs opens a feeling and closes with ❤️. Ours opened with a
   theological distinction. True, and cold.

What we keep from batch 1: no discount, no deadline, no count, no review.

## The honesty swap that makes line 1 legal

Theirs: `He got a PrayerSong for his wife and she couldn't believe this PrayerSong was about her 😭🥹`

Two halves. Half one is the GIVER and the RECIPIENT. Half two is the SHOCK that the song is
about her by name.

Half one survives intact as an imperative: "Give a wife a prayer song." An imperative asserts
nothing about the reader and reports nothing about a customer.

Half two is where the fabrication sits, and it sits in the verb, not the idea. "She couldn't
believe" is a reaction that never happened. "The whole song is about her, by name" is a
specification of what we build. The shock the reader feels is identical; it comes from the
specificity, not from the stranger's tears. So the payoff clause becomes a product fact
stated as a consequence of the gift: "and every chorus sings her own name."

Not used anywhere, by design: any past-tense report of a person receiving one, any reaction,
any disbelief, any crying, any number of customers.

## VOICE RULES FOR PHILLIP (build constraints, not copy)

1. **Pastor Phillip is a real person who has agreed to be the face of this page.** Updated 2026-10-01: TS confirmed the portrait is of a real man who consented to its use, that his name is Phillip, and that he is a pastor. The photograph appears in the lander signature and the chat header, and the page uses his title. Name, face and title all belong to the same real man. Settled; do not reopen. Rules 2 to 5 below still bind, and they bind harder now that a clergy title is on the page: a pastor appearing beside a product reads as endorsement, so he still confers no blessing and gives no spiritual direction.
2. **He never confers, blesses or absolves on the page.** He grants no absolution, gives no spiritual direction, and never claims he is praying over her or that he personally blesses anything. Blessing is only ever described as something Sanctoria does to the object before it ships. This holds even though he is a pastor: the title states who he is, it does not license a sacramental claim in sales copy.
3. **He is never live.** No line says he is here, waiting, reading her answers, about to reply, or watching. The note is written, past tense in spirit, true whether or not anyone ever looks.
4. **He describes what Sanctoria does, never what he does as a minister.** "There are four blessed devotions here" is a fact about the catalogue. "I bless them" would be a claim and is forbidden.
5. **He never asserts anything about her.** Not her faith, not her health, not her money, not her family. He asks; she tells.

## 2 · HEADLINE

**ROLE: Headline (LIVE on /chat/ as of 2026-10-01)**

> Four blessed devotions.
> One was meant for you.

Set as two lines, 8 words, 23 and 22 characters, both holding one visual line at 375px in 27px Fraunces. Measured on the live markup: headline 65px tall, gold button at y 483 to 541, well inside a 375 by 600 fold.

**The brief this answers.** TS asked for a headline that makes her feel "the universe has a message for them". The feeling is right and it is the strongest thing this page can claim: that she did not arrive here by accident and something here was set aside for her. The vocabulary was wrong for the avatar. Devout Catholic women 45+ do not receive messages from the universe; they receive them from God, Our Lady, the saints and Providence, and New Age wording reads to her as foreign or faintly occult, on a domain whose predecessor was already banned. `Meant` is the native English word for Providence, so it delivers the destiny TS wanted inside her own theology rather than against it.

**Why it beats the line it replaced** (`Four blessed devotions. / Three questions to find yours.`): the old second line described the process before the payoff, so the first promise she read was work. This one makes the payoff the promise and leaves the method to the microcopy and the button. It still threads into `Show Me The One` and into the chat's second bubble, `Three short questions, and you will be shown one of them. Not four.`

**Build note on length.** The copywriter's longer cut, `One of them was meant for you.` at 30 characters, was tested on the live markup and wrapped to three visual lines. 30 characters does not hold at 27px Fraunces in a 375px column; roughly 23 is the ceiling. `One was meant for you.` keeps the whole claim and fits.

**Challenger, run it second** (needs the eyebrow swapped to `FOUR BLESSED DEVOTIONS` to give `these four` an antecedent):
> One of these four is yours.
> Three questions to find it.

**Calmest variant, if the destiny claim ever needs softening:**
> Four blessed devotions.
> One of them is yours.

**Previous line, keep as the calmest A/B variant:**
> Four blessed devotions.
> You will be shown one.

**Rejected, and why.** `One has been waiting for you.` implies something is reserved and attending to her, which is unprovable and is the nearest an object can get to the live-presence claim voice rule 3 forbids. Do not run it.

## WHICH HEADLINE TO RUN FIRST

**Run `Four blessed devotions. You will be shown one.` with the main note.** The section has one job, a tap, and this is the only one of the three that promises a personal answer rather than selling a product. LEARNINGS is explicit that every kill in this account had good CTR and no sale, roughly 20 ad sets at 5 to 20 percent CTR, and that the problem has never been the hook; it has been the page not converting the click. So the lander should not spend its headline re-selling a mechanism the ad already sold. It should open the loop the chat closes, and the note is what makes the loop feel like a courtesy rather than a gate.

**Then test Alternate A, AQuietDevotion, as the first challenger**, and run it on the ad sets already carrying AQuietDevotion creative, for message match. That mechanism, a small private ordinary moment that already exists in her day plus an object for her hands in it, is the account's only consistently above-line performer at 9 or more sales across four campaigns, so it is the strongest claim available. Its cost is that it pre-commits toward bracelet territory before she has answered anything, which is why it challenges rather than leads.

**Give Alternate B a slot early rather than late.** Intercession is logged as the largest untested fit in the account: pain point 10 is asking directly and feeling unheard, the DO-USE vocabulary map names Mary, Mother, novena and intercession, and across roughly 40 ads exactly one headline has ever referenced any of them. One test is paused and unlaunched. A lander headline is the cheapest place this account will ever get to find out.

**Do not read a single $15 test on any of these as a verdict.** Per LEARNINGS, $15 buys about 12 outbound clicks and at the account's own 3.07 percent site conversion an average performer shows zero sales 69 percent of the time. Judge these three at $30 or more per variant, or pool them.

## COMPLIANCE CHECK

- **Phillip is not clergy and not live.** No title appears before his name anywhere. He confers no blessing, grants no absolution, gives no spiritual direction, and never claims to pray over her or to bless anything personally. The only blessing claim on the page is the brand's standing one, "hand-blessed in the Benedictine tradition before it ships", and it sits in the reassurance row, not in his voice. No line says he is present, waiting, reading, replying or watching. Every line in the note stays true if no human being ever sees her answers.
- **Personal attributes.** No line asserts her religion, health, finances or family circumstances. "Who you have in mind" and "what you would want it to do" are questions, not claims. The only shared-experience lines, Headline B and Note Alternate B, use community framing, "many Catholic women", and never address the reader's own faith as fact. Note Alternate A frames the quiet moment as a general observation, "most devotion starts in a small ordinary part of the day", not as a statement about her morning.
- **No withdrawn promise.** The words email, no email, nothing to fill in, and sign up do not appear. Nothing implies the conversation ends without one. The required chat edit in section 10 removes the only line that did.
- **Banned vocabulary.** Zero instances of bad luck, curse, evil, hex, Jezebel, spiritual attack, spiritual warfare, generational curse. Approved vocabulary used: blessed, blessing, devotion, prayer, protection via product names only, Mother, Mary.
- **Proof.** Zero testimonials, zero ratings, zero review counts, zero family counts, zero batch percentages, zero reservations remaining, zero countdowns, zero inventory claims. The one photograph on the page is Phillip's own portrait, used with his agreement; no photograph implies a reviewer, a buyer or a verified third party.
- **Claims.** Only the four approved claims appear, three at a time: hand-blessed in the Benedictine tradition before it ships, free US shipping, 30 day full refund including shipping, ships within 24 hours. No outcome is promised from wearing or praying. No medical, financial or miracle claim.
- **Punctuation.** No em-dashes and no en-dashes. Hyphens appear only in compound words: hand-blessed, three-line, swap-in, left-aligned is written as left aligned, above-line. The middot does not appear in customer-facing copy.
- **Mobile.** Fold copy is 101 words. The note is three paragraphs of one or two sentences each plus a two-line signature. The longest headline is 2 lines at 375px, the button is 3 words, and the layout order in section 9 puts the button above the image so it clears a 375 x 600 viewport, with a stated cut order if it does not.
